Wolfenstein 3D for the ESP32-S31-Korvo-1 (esp32-s31-linux be5b725)
===================================================================

Status
======
The first version ran on the board on 2026-10-08: it started first go, with and without
--resf 800 480. With --resf 800 480 it ended a game with
     wolf3d: 941 frames of the 3D view, 150.9 s of drawing (6.2 fps)

This is the second version. It puts the picture on the screen in another way, to get
that frame rate up (see "Picture" below). Everything else is as it was in the first.
It ran on the board on 2026-10-09, and with --timedemo 0 gave
     (none)                          35.3 frames a second
     --scale fill                    29.7
     --scale off --res 640 400        8.1
The last of those is the first version's way of drawing, in this binary. So the
default is 4.4 times as fast as it was, and --scale fill, which covers the whole
panel, 3.7 times as fast as the first version's 640x400.

There are two builds of it from the same sources: wolf3d-korvo, which is the one that
gave those figures, and wolf3d-korvo-zb, which lets the compiler use the processor's
Zba, Zbb and Zbs instructions (see "The Zb build" below). That one has not run on the
board.

All were built with the board's toolchain and tested under qemu-riscv32-static: the
binary itself with the real game data, and SDL's frame buffer driver and the game
against a fake console (a frame buffer with one mode, virtual terminals, event
devices). Both patches of the first version were read by a second reviewer against
the board's kernel source, and what that found is fixed; the new picture code has not
had that second reading. Apart from the four frame rates above, nothing in this file
about how it looks, feels or performs on the panel has been reported from hardware.
What is still to be confirmed is listed at the end.

What it is
==========
Wolf4SDL, the SDL port of the original game, at the last revision that uses SDL 1.2,
linked statically with SDL 1.2.15 and SDL_mixer 1.2.12. SDL's own frame buffer console
driver ("fbcon") puts the picture on /dev/fb0 and reads the keyboard from the console,
so nothing has to be installed on the board. One patch changes SDL and one the game.
The game data is not here and must not be: it is commercial. You copy your own.

Running it
==========
TEST-PLAN.txt has the steps in a careful order. In short: put wolf3d-korvo and the eight
WL6 files in a folder "wolf3d" on the card's FAT partition, then on the panel
     cp /mnt/sd/wolf3d/wolf3d-korvo /root/wolf3d2
     chmod +x /root/wolf3d2
     cd /mnt/sd/wolf3d
     /root/wolf3d2
That installs this version as /root/wolf3d2 and leaves the first at /root/wolf3d.
The data has to be the full version 1.4 as GT Interactive and Activision sold it, which
is what Steam has: VSWAP.WL6 is 1,544,376 bytes and VGAGRAPH.WL6 275,774. Other
versions are refused with a message ("Wolf4SDL was not compiled for these data
files"); they need a rebuild with other lines in version.h. The files are looked for in
the current directory, in lower case first and then in upper case.
Settings and saved games go in /root/.wolf4sdl/.

Options
=======
(none)               draws 320x200, as the original did, and shows it doubled: 640x400
                     in the middle of the panel
--scale fill         the same picture stretched to the whole panel, 800x480
--scale aspect       the same picture at 640x480: the shape it had on a monitor
--scale off          not enlarged, and put on the screen by SDL as in the first
                     version: 320x200 in the middle
--res 640 400        draws at 640x400: finer walls, and more than three times the
                     work. With --scale off this is the first version's default.
--resf 400 240       draws at 400x240 and shows it doubled, which is the whole panel
                     with even pixels: a quarter finer than the default for a third
                     more work. Menus and full-screen pictures are 640x400 in the top
                     left corner.
--resf 800 480       draws at 800x480: the most work. Menus and full-screen pictures
                     stay 640x400, in the top left corner.
--screen W H         the size of the screen, where SDL cannot tell (the dummy driver)
--timedemo N         plays demo N (0 to 3) as fast as it will go, prints the frame rate
                     and ends. The time includes the fade-in at the start of the demo,
                     which is thirty redraws of the picture and a quarter of a second
                     of waiting; any key stops it early.
--showfps            the frame rate in the top left corner while playing
--nowait             no "Press a key" on the way in
--tedlevel N         starts in a level: 0 to 9 are the floors of episode 1, 10 to 19 of
                     episode 2, and so on
--fix-console        puts the console on the panel back in text mode if a killed game
                     left it in graphics mode, and does nothing else. For another
                     login, a timer, or typing blind.
--help               the rest (joystick, sound rate, configuration directory)

In the environment:
SDL_NOMOUSE=1          do not look for a mouse
SDL_FBCON_NEWVT=1      run on a new virtual terminal, as SDL did before the patch
SDL_FBCON_VTSWITCH=1   let Ctrl+Alt+F1 to F12 switch terminals, as SDL did before
SDL_FBCON_VERBOSE=     (empty) do not print the "SDL fbcon:" lines
WOLF3D_NOGUARD=1       do not start the console guard
SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy  no screen at all: for --timedemo without the panel
WOLF3D_CHECK_PRESENT=1 for tests: read the screen back after every picture and stop
                       if it is not what was drawn. Slow.

Every run that enlarged the picture says so at the end, for example
     wolf3d: drawn at 320x200, shown at 640x400 from (80,40)

Every run that drew the 3D view ends with a line such as
     wolf3d: 1830 frames of the 3D view, 61.0 s of drawing (30.0 fps)
A frame that follows the one before within a second counts; a longer gap is a menu or a
level being loaded and is left out. In play the game draws at most 70 frames a second,
and the demos behind the title screen at most 17.5.

Keys
====
Arrows                move and turn
Ctrl                  fire
Alt                   strafe (hold, with the left and right arrows)
Shift                 run
Space                 open doors, use
1 to 4                weapons
Esc                   menu
Enter, Y, N           select, confirm
F10, then Y           quit
Alt+F4                quit at once, without asking
These are the game's own. Right Ctrl, Right Alt and Right Shift work as the left ones.
Ctrl+C does nothing while the game runs: the keyboard is in raw mode.

Mouse
=====
SDL looks at /dev/input/event0 to event31 when it starts and uses up to four devices
that report relative X and Y movement and have a left button. It prints one line for
each, "SDL fbcon: mouse on /dev/input/event1 (name)", or "SDL fbcon: no mouse found".
Left and right turn, forward and back walk, the left button fires, the right button
strafes while held, the middle button is "use". Sensitivity is in the Control menu.
The mouse has to be plugged in before the game starts. One that is unplugged while
playing is dropped and the game carries on; it is not looked for again.
SDL as released could not have used a mouse here at all: it reads /dev/input/mice and
the like, which this kernel does not have (no CONFIG_INPUT_MOUSEDEV), and it refuses to
start when it finds none.

Sound
=====
There is none: the board has no sound device. The game prints "No sound: No available
audio device" at the end of each run and plays without. Wolf4SDL as it stands ends at
start-up in that case ("SD_PrepareSound(0): DigiList not initialized!"); that is one
of the things the patch changes. The sound code is still all there, and ran under the
emulator with SDL's dummy sound device. If the board ever gets a device that SDL 1.2 can
open (/dev/dsp), memory becomes the problem: the game converts every sound effect to
the output rate at start-up. Under the emulator it then asked for 17 MiB at the
default rate of 44100, which is more than the board has, and 6.4 MiB with
"--samplerate 11025".

Picture
=======
The first version was slow on the board: 6 to 8 frames a second. Where the work went
was then measured, by building the same sources for the PC and running demo 0 under
valgrind's cachegrind, which counts instructions and stores. For one frame:

  first version                      instructions   stores to memory
  640x400, its default                  3,720,000        478,000
  --resf 800 480                        5,330,000        704,000
  --res 320 200 (a small picture)       1,180,000        132,000

Of the stores at 640x400, 58 in 100 were SDL turning the whole picture into screen
pixels, one pixel to a store, every frame, changed or not; 25 in 100 were the walls,
drawn at four times the original's pixels. On this board, with the panel on, the port
runs its cache write-through: every store goes straight to the PSRAM. So stores had
to be a large part of what a frame costs. How large, the board has since said: see
"What the board says" below. fbDOOM gets its 18 frames a second by drawing 320x200
and enlarging that as it goes to the screen, two pixels to a store.

This version does the same, and one thing more. The game draws at 320x200 unless --res
says otherwise. On the way to the screen the picture is enlarged; a copy of what is on
the screen is kept, the new picture is compared with it four pixels at a time, and
only what differs is written, two screen pixels to a store (id_vp.cpp). The ceiling,
the floor, the status bar and anything else that did not change cost a comparison and
no store. The same demo:

  this version                       instructions   stores to memory   picture
  (none)                                1,080,000         83,000       640x400 at (80,40)
  --scale aspect                        1,080,000         85,000       640x480 at (80,0)
  --scale fill                          1,200,000        101,000       800x480
  --resf 400 240                        1,450,000        113,000       800x480
  --res 640 400                         3,440,000        275,000       640x400 at (80,40)
  --resf 800 480                        4,890,000        397,000       800x480
  --scale off --res 640 400             3,720,000        478,000       the first version

These are counts on a PC, of x86 code, and they are not times. The board's code was
counted for three of the rows in another way (the emulator's log of every instruction
it carries out) and came to 1,065,000, 1,163,000 and 1,436,000 instructions a frame
for the default, --scale fill and --resf 400 240: within 3 in 100 of the PC's. Stores
could not be counted that way. One place where they are known to flatter: the PC
clears the ceiling and floor some 20 bytes to a store and the board 4, which is about
9,000 more stores a frame at 320x200 than the table shows, and more in proportion at
the larger sizes. The default now takes under a fifth of the stores and under a third
of the instructions that it took, and --scale fill a seventh of the stores of the
first version's --resf 800 480.

What the board says
-------------------
Three of the rows have been timed on the board, with --timedemo 0:

                                     frames a second    ms a frame
  (none)                                  35.3              28.3
  --scale fill                            29.7              33.7
  --scale off --res 640 400                8.1             123.5

Suppose a frame costs so much for each instruction and so much for each store. The
first and the third row then give 16 ns an instruction and 135 ns a store, and the
second is a check on that: it should take 32.7 ms, and took 33.7. So the counts are a
fair guide to this board, and both things cost. In the default mode a frame is about
17 ms of instructions and 11 ms of stores; in the first version's it was 59 and 64.

16 ns is five ticks of a 320 MHz clock for an average instruction, which is a lot,
and the counts do not say where it goes. Loads that miss the cache are the likely
place: the picture that is compared and the copy it is compared with are 64 KB each,
and the data cache is 64 KB in all. The stores of the ceiling and floor that the PC
undercounts are in there too. tools/storecost.c is a small program for the board
that times loads and stores of each kind on their own; TEST-PLAN.txt has how to run
it. It has run under the emulator only, where it shows nothing of interest.

By those two figures the rows that have not been timed come to

                                     frames a second
  --scale aspect                          35
  --resf 400 240                          26
  --res 640 400                           11
  --resf 800 480                           7.6
  first version, --resf 800 480            5.6     (6.2 measured, in play)

These are worked out, not measured, and the timed demo includes a fade at its start
that costs the faster modes a few in 100. What they say is plain enough: the two ways
of filling the panel from a small picture give 25 frames a second or more, and
drawing at the panel's own 800x480 does not come near. Every wall pixel drawn is a
store and a handful of instructions, the walls at 800x480 are six times the pixels of
the walls at 320x200, and nothing in how the picture is put on the screen changes
that.

So for a full panel there are two choices. --scale fill stretches the 320x200 picture,
menus included, with pixels two or three panel pixels wide by turns. --resf 400 240
draws a wider and finer view and doubles it exactly, with the menus in the top left
corner at 640x400.

--scale fill is also the first mode in which menus and full-screen pictures fill the
panel: they are part of the 320x200 picture that is stretched. The picture is then
wider than it was drawn (5:3 from 8:5), and its pixels are two or three panel pixels
wide by turns. --scale aspect has the shape the game had on a 4:3 monitor, with pixels
of even width.

The picture is written straight into the visible screen. There is no vsync and no
second page, so tearing is possible, as in fbDOOM. A fade redraws all of it at each of
its thirty steps, as before, but each step is now a little over half the stores it was.

A correction to the first version of this file. It gave the cost of 640x400 as 1.7
times that of 320x200 and of 800x480 as 2.0 times, from how long the emulator took.
The emulator's time is mostly its own overhead per frame, and the counts above are
what should have been measured: 3.2 and 4.5 times the instructions, 3.6 and 5.3 times
the stores.

The Zb build
============
The board's device tree lists three extensions for the processor that Linux runs on,
beyond the RV32IMAC that the board's toolchain builds for: Zba (shift and add, for
working out addresses), Zbb (minimum and maximum, counting bits, swapping bytes,
widening) and Zbs (single bits). Nothing on the board uses them. wolf3d-korvo-zb is
the game, SDL and SDL_mixer compiled with
     -march=rv32imac_zicsr_zifencei_zba_zbb_zbs -mabi=ilp32
The C library and the compiler's own routines that are linked in are the toolchain's,
without them. The compiler found about 2,900 places for such an instruction in the
binary, nearly half of them sh2add and sh1add.

What it saves was counted in the emulator's log of every instruction it carries out,
over a whole run of --timedemo 0, start-up included:

                         wolf3d-korvo    wolf3d-korvo-zb    fewer, in 100
  (none)                  736,142,113      714,206,666           3.0
  --scale fill            803,563,253      772,824,923           3.8
  --resf 400 240          992,303,789      963,448,679           2.9

Stores were not counted for it; nothing in these extensions is about stores, so they
should be the same. By the two figures from the board, instructions are about three
fifths of a frame, so this should be worth some 2 in 100: half a frame a second at
30. That is a guess until the board has run it, and not worth much either way.

A processor without one of the three would stop at the first such instruction it
came to, wherever that was. So this build tries one instruction of each kind before
it does anything else, and if one is refused it prints
     wolf3d: this build is for a processor with Zbb, and this one does not have it.
     Use the build without "zb" in its name.
and ends with status 1, with the console as it was. "Before anything else" turned out
to mean before the constructors of the program's static objects, which run ahead of
main(): the first such instruction is in one of them, the OPL emulator's tables. This
was tried under the emulator with each of the three turned off in turn, and is a case
in the tests. --fix-console in this build makes the same check, so on such a
processor that is a job for the other build too.

Memory
======
The game asks the kernel for 1.8 MiB while playing a demo in its default mode and with
--scale fill, and 2.2 MiB with --res 640 400 (the first version: 2.0 MiB); 1.5 MiB of
that is VSWAP.WL6, which it reads whole. The binary adds 0.57 MiB of code and 0.33 MiB
of data. That is from the emulator's system call trace, not from the board.

The console
===========
While it runs, the game has the panel's console in graphics mode and the keyboard in
raw mode. What puts them back, and when:

* Any ordinary end (Quit, Alt+F4, an error, the end of a timed demo): SDL, before
  anything is printed. Messages from while the game ran are printed after that.
* A signal that would end the game (SEGV, BUS, ILL, FPE, ABRT, TERM, HUP, INT, QUIT,
  PIPE, USR1, USR2), or a crash from the stack running out: SDL's frame buffer driver,
  in the signal handler, with system calls only. So "killall wolf3d" is safe. SIGALRM
  is ignored, as SDL sets it. If the terminal has been hung up (the shell that owned
  it has gone), the handler opens it again to put it back.
* exit() from anywhere without SDL being told: the same code, from an atexit()
  function.
* The game being killed outright (kill -9, or the kernel when memory runs out) or
  ending through _exit(): the console guard. This is a second process, the same
  binary started with --console-guard, that holds one end of a pipe and does nothing
  until the other end closes, which it does when the game is gone however that
  happened. It then looks at the console and puts it back if it is still in graphics
  mode or raw. Until then it sleeps. Under the emulator it asked the kernel for no
  memory at all; what it holds on the board (a stack and the few pages of the binary
  it runs) has not been measured.
* By hand, with no game running: /root/wolf3d --fix-console. From another login, or
  from a timer started on the panel before the game, as step 3 of TEST-PLAN.txt does.
  It puts right graphics mode, a raw keyboard and SDL's terminal settings on the
  terminal that is on the screen.

What none of this covers:
* "killall -9 wolf3d" kills the guard with the game. Kill the game alone.
* A game that hangs without ending. No key reaches it. Only a timer started
  beforehand or another login can end it; then the above applies.
* After a kill -9, a shell that read the terminal settings before the guard put them
  back keeps restoring the game's: programs then do not show what is typed and Ctrl+C
  does nothing. "stty sane" puts that right, and so does --fix-console. The guard runs
  at a real-time priority to get there first; on one processor it should. Not seen on
  the board either way.
* From another login or with SDL_FBCON_NEWVT, SDL waits for the screen to reach its new
  terminal, and waits for ever if another program has the screen in graphics mode. That
  is SDL as released, and cannot happen when the game is started on the panel.

The game stays on the virtual terminal it was started from (tty1). SDL as released
would have moved it to a new one, tty2, and back at the end.

What the patches change
=======================
SDL (725 lines added, 39 removed, five files; all in the fbcon driver but one line):
* One request to find out that the frame buffer has a single mode, in place of sixty.
  This matters more than it sounds: in text mode the kernel redraws the whole console
  after each request that succeeds.
* Start-up without a mouse.
* Mice on /dev/input/eventN (new file).
* The game stays on its own virtual terminal.
* Signals and exit() put the console back, as above, and SIGINT and SIGTERM work again
  once SDL has quit.
* A video mode that cannot be set leaves the console in text mode for the message.
* Ctrl+Alt+Fn no longer switches terminals unless asked: it is fire, strafe and a
  function key.
* Multimedia keys (keycodes above 127) are no longer read as three other keys.
* Only the keymaps the kernel has are fetched.
* A small surface no longer takes the memory of a screen-wide one.
* The whole screen is cleared at the end, not just the top 400 lines.
* A cast, from SDL's own later history, so that it compiles with GCC 15.

What that comes to at start-up, counted against the fake console (tests/results.txt):

                                   as released     patched
  mode requests to the kernel      62              3
  console redraws caused           61              2
  keymap requests                  65,536          2,048
  virtual terminal switches        2               0
  starts without /dev/input/mice   no              yes

The game (1,247 lines added, 50 removed, seventeen files):
* Carries on with no sound device.
* Draws at 320x200 and enlarges the picture itself on the way to the screen, writing
  only what changed (new file, id_vp.cpp). See "Picture" above.
* Draws straight into the frame buffer. As released it asks for double buffering, which
  this frame buffer cannot do, and SDL then keeps a second copy of the screen and
  copies it across on every frame.
* Finds data files with upper case names.
* --timedemo, --showfps, and the frame rate line at the end.
* Puts the console back before it prints an error; keeps other messages for the end.
* The console guard and --fix-console (new file, wl_fbcon.cpp).
* In a build for Zba, Zbb or Zbs, a check before anything else that the processor has
  them. It is not compiled into the plain build.

Toolchain
=========
The board's own Buildroot 2026.08 toolchain: GCC 15.3.0, riscv32-buildroot-linux-musl,
musl 1.2.6, rv32imac, ilp32 (soft-float). It is used by absolute path from the finished
build in ~/esp32-s31-linux-main. Nothing in that tree is changed. -O2 throughout, as
Buildroot builds the rest of the board's software. The Zb build uses the same
compiler and the same libraries with a wider -march for its own code.

Build (WSL2 Debian)
===================
     bash build.sh [WORKDIR]
WORKDIR defaults to ~/wolf-scratch/build. It needs curl, git, patch and make, and
fetches about 10 MB. It checks the two tarballs against the checksums above, applies
the patches, builds the two libraries into WORKDIR/prefix and the game against them,
and leaves WORKDIR/wolf3d-korvo. It takes about half a minute on this PC.

     VARIANT=zb bash build.sh [WORKDIR]
does the same with "-march=rv32imac_zicsr_zifencei_zba_zbb_zbs -mabi=ilp32" added to
the compiler's flags for the two libraries and the game, in ~/wolf-scratch/build-zb
unless told otherwise, and leaves WORKDIR/wolf3d-korvo-zb.

Each was run twice from nothing, in two different directories, and gave the same file
both times, byte for byte: the checksums at the top.

What it does, for doing it by hand:
* SDL and SDL_mixer: config.sub and config.guess in build-scripts/ are replaced with
  current ones (Buildroot's, from support/gnuconfig); the ones in the tarballs are from
  2012 and do not know riscv32. Then ./configure --host=riscv32-buildroot-linux-musl
  --disable-shared with CFLAGS="-O2 -std=gnu99", and for SDL every video driver off
  but fbcon and dummy, no assembly, and --enable-clock_gettime so that the game's clock
  does not jump when the board's clock is set. The full list is in build.sh.
* The game: "make GPL=1" with CXX and CC set, CFLAGS="" and LDFLAGS="-static" in the
  environment (as arguments to make they would replace the Makefile's own), and
  LDFLAGS_SDL="-L.../prefix/lib -lSDL_mixer -lSDL -lpthread -lm": in a static link
  SDL_mixer has to come before SDL. Then strip.
* version.h is left as it is: GOODTIMES and CARMACIZED, which is this data.

Tests (under qemu-riscv32-static, not on the board)
===================================================
     bash tests/run-tests.sh
It needs the trees that build.sh leaves in WORKDIR, python3, and a directory with the
eight WL6 files (DATA; they are not part of this). Start it in the foreground and not
under nohup: a signal that is ignored when it starts stays ignored in the programs it
tests, which is right, and the cases for that signal then have less to check. It takes
about half an hour, most of it the broken copies. The last run is in
tests/results.txt: 89 cases and 36 broken copies, 125 checks, all passed. The 89
cases were then run against the Zb build, with ARCH set as the top of run-tests.sh
says, and all passed: tests/results-zb.txt. The broken copies were not built a second
time for it; they test the tests.

* fakefb.c is the fake console. The files that talk to the console (three of SDL's, one
  of the game's) are compiled as they are, with open(), ioctl(), tcsetattr() and eight
  more renamed to its functions. Every other object in the test programs is the one in
  the board's binary. The fake frame buffer answers as simplefb does in the board's
  kernel: it takes any mode request and returns the mode it has. The fake terminals
  refuse to switch while one is in graphics mode or switching is locked, as the kernel
  does. Their state is kept in a file, so it can be read after the program has gone,
  whatever ended it, and so that the console guard, a second process, sees the same
  console. How each call should answer was read from the board's kernel source.
* sdlprobe.c is a small SDL program: the driver without the game. With it: where the
  picture lands for four sizes (every pixel of the fake screen is looked at), a size
  that does not fit, the start-up counts in the table above, keys (including a
  multimedia key while Ctrl is held, and Ctrl+Alt+F2), keys left unread, mouse
  movement, buttons and wheel, a mouse that goes away, SDL_NOMOUSE, a program that
  returns or calls exit() without SDL_Quit(), SIGTERM after SDL_Quit(), a start from a
  terminal that is not a console (as root and not), each of six system calls made to
  fail, each signal, and a signal after the terminal has been hung up.
* The picture. In every case below the game reads the screen back after each picture
  it puts there and stops if any pixel is not the one it drew, worked out from the
  colours and the picture afresh (WOLF3D_CHECK_PRESENT). Beside that: a still screen
  is captured unscaled, as SDL draws it, and enlarged in each of the three ways, and
  a script works out on its own what every one of the 384,000 pixels of the enlarged
  screen has to be; drawn at 640x400 and at 800x480 the screen has to be the same,
  byte for byte, as SDL's own code makes it; and the player is killed with the debug
  keys, which is a fizzle fade to red and one back into the level, in two modes.
* The game: play in six picture modes with a screen shot of each, the sign-on screen,
  title and menus, Quit from the menu, a timed demo to its end and stopped early, each
  signal, kill -9 with and without the guard, from a terminal that is not a console,
  half way into graphics mode, and with the screen on another terminal,
  --fix-console from another login and as a background job of the dead terminal, a
  store through a null pointer, abort(), a stack overflow, exit() and _exit() from
  inside, a data file that is missing (two ways the game ends), a mode that does not
  fit, graphics mode refused, and the mouse turning the view. After each one the
  console has to be as it was, by the steps that put it back and in their order, and
  nothing may have been printed while it was in graphics mode.
* Then thirty-six broken copies: one thing taken out of the source or put wrong each
  time (the signal handler, its stack, the atexit() function, the guard; a store in
  each of the row loops, where the picture is sampled, the copy of the screen, and so
  on). Each has to build, and at least one case has to fail for each. They all do.
* Two things the fake console cannot show and that were read from the kernel source
  only: that the guard gets to the terminal settings before the shell does, and that
  a guard which could not be started does not take the game with it.
* The binary itself: --help, no data, no console, all four demos to the end with the
  real data and no sound device (691, 1899, 1140 and 1656 frames, the same every
  time), one with SDL's dummy sound device, and one in each of the three enlarged
  modes into memory, with the read-back check on. And the demo on an emulated
  processor without Zba, without Zbb and without Zbs: the plain build has to play it,
  and the Zb build has to name the missing one and end with status 1.

Screen shots from the tests are left in WORKDIR/tests/shots. They are pictures of the
game, so they are not kept here.

Not yet checked on the board
============================
Known from the board: the first version starts, puts a picture on the panel with and
without --resf 800 480, and manages 6.2 frames a second with it; the second version
plays the timed demo to its end in three modes, at the rates given at the top. Nothing
below has been reported yet, for either version.
* How the picture modes look: whether anything is left behind when the view moves,
  the fades, the fizzle fade when the player dies.
* The frame rates of --scale aspect, --resf 400 240, --res 640 400, --resf 800 480
  and of the game with no screen at all.
* What a load and a store cost, each on its own: tools/storecost.c.
* The Zb build: that the processor carries out the three kinds of instruction (the
  device tree lists them, and the build tries one of each before anything else), and
  whether it is any faster.
* Tearing, and whether the sign-on and menu screens fade in and out cleanly. Each step
  of a fade redraws the whole picture.
* The keys, on the real keyboard through the real kernel keymap.
* The mouse: finding it, and how turning feels.
* That the console comes back cleanly after each kind of exit, and that the lines
  printed at the end can be read. All of this was tested against the fake console only.
* The console guard: that it starts (it runs /proc/self/exe), that it is not in the
  way, and that it does its job after a kill -9. Its real-time priority is untried.
* Memory: that 2 MiB and the binary fit beside everything else on the board.
* A USB game controller. SDL's joystick code is in the binary and would look for one;
  none of that was tested. "--joystick -1" turns it off if it causes trouble.
